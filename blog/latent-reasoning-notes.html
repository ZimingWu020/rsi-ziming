Latent reasoning notes: why replace discrete CoT

2026.11.04 · 8 min · Sample post — replace with your writing

Discrete chain-of-thought writes reasoning as readable tokens. That helps humans inspect the trail and makes supervised alignment easier. It also forces intermediate computation into language bandwidth: many steps don’t need to be said out loud, yet they still occupy context.

Motivation

Latent reasoning tries to advance intermediate steps in a continuous hidden space and decode only when something must be delivered outward. Potential gains include less surface filler, longer effective trajectories, and a more natural interface to non-linguistic signals such as vision or action.

Boundaries to keep

  • Verifiability: without a text trail, evaluation and debugging need task contracts and process probes.
  • Training stability: drifting hidden states can turn “looks like thinking” into uncontrolled noise.
  • Tool interfaces: external actions still need explicit discrete events.

When it belongs in the queue

When the bottleneck is clearly “intermediate compute is too long or too expensive,” not “can’t use tools” or “can’t read docs,” latent methods are worth scheduling. Otherwise harden harness, evaluation, and data contracts first.
